"What is VAT?"
"VAT (Value Added Tax) is a consumption tax levied on the value added to goods or services at each stage of the supply chain. It is ultimately borne by the end consumer, and businesses remit it to the government."
"How do I calculate the gross price from a net price?"
"Gross price = Net price × (1 + VAT rate). If the net price is $100 and VAT is 20%: gross price = 100 × 1.20 = $120."
"How do I extract the net price from a gross price?"
"Net price = Gross price ÷ (1 + VAT rate). If the gross price is $120 and VAT is 20%: net price = 120 ÷ 1.20 = $100."
"What is the difference between VAT and GST?"
"GST (Goods and Services Tax) is a single-stage tax collected at the point of final sale. VAT is a multi-stage tax collected at each stage of the supply chain, with credits along the way. Both function similarly but differ in structure."

"What is the break-even point?"
"The break-even point is where total revenue equals total costs - no profit and no loss. Above it, the business is profitable; below it, the business loses money."
"How do I calculate the break-even point (in units)?"
"Break-even units = Fixed costs ÷ (Selling price - Variable cost per unit). If fixed costs are $10,000, the selling price is $50, and variable costs are $30: break-even = 10,000 ÷ (50 - 30) = 500 units."
"What is the difference between fixed and variable costs?"
"Fixed costs do not change with production levels (rent, insurance, salaries). Variable costs change with production (raw materials, direct labor, shipping). Identifying both is essential for an accurate break-even analysis."
"Why is break-even analysis important?"
"It tells you how much you need to sell to start making a profit, informing pricing, cost control, and business planning. It helps evaluate the viability of new products or the impact of changes on profitability."

"What is APR?"
"APR (Annual Percentage Rate) is the true annual cost of a loan expressed as a rate. It includes the interest rate plus fees (origination fees, closing costs, etc.), providing a single comparable number."
"What is the difference between APR and the interest rate?"
"The interest rate is your cost of borrowing. APR includes the interest rate plus loan fees (origination fees, points, mortgage insurance, closing costs). APR is always equal to or higher than the interest rate and gives the full cost picture."
"Why is APR important for comparing loans?"
"Lenders may offer a low rate with high fees, or a high rate with low fees. APR combines both into one number, making different loan products from different lenders truly comparable."
"Does APR account for early repayment?"
"No. APR assumes you hold the loan for its full term. If you pay off early or refinance, the actual cost may be higher because you pay the same fees over a shorter period."

"What is the difference between short-term and long-term capital gains?"
"Short-term gains apply to assets held for less than a year and are taxed at ordinary income rates (10-37%). Long-term gains apply to assets held for more than a year and are taxed at lower rates (0%, 15%, or 20%)."
"What is the Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT)?"
"NIIT is an additional 3.8% tax on investment income for high earners (over $200,000 single, $250,000 married filing jointly). It is added on top of regular capital gains tax."
"How do capital gains interact with my other income?"
